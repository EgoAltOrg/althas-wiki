## Overview

The Basilica of Saint Avel stands in the center of [[jestha|Jestha]]. It is the [[parish-of-orthodoxy|Parish of Orthodoxy]]'s base in [[jesthaen|Jesthaen]]. A tree-lined path leads to a complex of chapel, infirmaries, cloister, medicinal herb gardens and orchards. Patients may stay for weeks or months, learn the rhythms of the house and help with small tasks as they recover.

The community lives its faith in [[the-one-above|the One Above]] through mercy and forgiveness, and it makes room for long care and lasting friendships.

### The three customs

- **The shared table.** At dusk, clergy, patients and guests dine together, with no seats reserved by rank. A brief prayer precedes the meal; conversation, requests for bread and small disagreements follow. Caregivers and those recovering share the same daily life.
- **Care first.** New arrivals receive water, a bed and care before anyone asks where they came from or what they have done. Confession is a later, voluntary possibility. The willingness to treat extends to people others would condemn, Sinners included (see [[the-holy-see|the Holy See]]). Offering treatment grants no absolution, changes no sentence and alters no rule of custody.
- **Leave something growing.** Those who recover are invited to plant something before they leave. Some trees are tended by people who never knew who planted them, and the gardens and orchards keep traces of generations of patients.

## History

The basilica grew from the small infirmary where [[avel-cruoris|Avel Cruoris]] first treated people during [[valerions-heresy|Valerion's Heresy]] (93 to 117 VR). Her disciples continued the work and built the basilica around it.

Its practice of treating Sinners before they show repentance has made the founder's mercy uncomfortable for some within the Church for as long as the house has stood.

## Notable Places

### The cloister and the core

The chapel, the main infirmaries and the refectory surround a central cloister garden with covered galleries. Paths and sheltered passages link this core to smaller buildings scattered through the gardens, and the herb gardens and orchards extend behind the complex.

### Avel's original infirmary

The small house where Avel first treated people is still part of the complex and still an infirmary: its beds are in use, its linen washed, its patients given everyday care. Beside the beds stands Avel's only likeness, a stained-glass window of a pale-haired figure in dark robes, hands joined in prayer against red glass. Its red light falls across the room where her work began.
