title: The Faunus

**Summary**: What little Althan scholars understand of the Wickling faith of the Witherwild. The Wicklings share their land with the Faunus and hold them to be gods; Althan scholars grant only that they are far greater than the ordinary life of the Fanewick. The account rests on one Haven-era missionary's records and the reports of Witherwatch veterans, and much of it is disputed.

---

Althan scholarship of the Fanewick faith rests on almost no first-hand material. Its one real source is a single [[house-aquila|House Aquila]] missionary who served in the [[haven|Haven]] colony and, bearing the [[miracles|Miracle of Pentecost]], could understand the Wicklings and record the beings they worship in words an Althan could read. That missionary named those beings the **Faunus** and fixed the forms of their names the mainland now uses; the Wicklings call them otherwise, in Wick. Those records and the less reliable reports of [[the-witherwatch|Witherwatch]] veterans make up nearly the whole archive, and modern scholars treat even them with caution. What follows is pieced together from that thin, much-disputed record, and nothing in it is settled.

## What the Wicklings call gods

The Wicklings hold the Faunus to be gods. Althan scholars are careful not to: none will say what the Faunus are, though none disputes that they are far greater than the ordinary life of the Fanewick. The records describe them as close at hand. They walk the land in the flesh, live in the villages, and turn up on the road as readily as any neighbor. A few great Faunus are attested across the whole country, chief among them the Great Owl Nikta, the Shepherd of the Seasons; the missionary held that every community, and even every family, keeps its own lesser Faunus besides, a guardian of that hearth or that stretch of ground, unknown ten miles away. On their number the sources give no consistent answer.

## The push and the pull

Worship, by the missionary's account, is a bargain worked from both sides. The Faunus court the Wicklings' devotion with small miracles and safe passage through their territories, and repay those who slight or trespass on them with tricks and curses. The Wicklings depend on those blessings to live in a land that would otherwise kill them, and render each Faunus its due, the offering and the observance it expects, tuned to its own nature. By Althan measure the faith lacks two things: a high power set above the rest, and a priesthood standing between worshipper and worshipped. A Wickling deals with the Faunus directly.

## The rules that keep them safe

What an Althan would name piety, the sources suggest a Wickling would name knowing the rules. The Fanewick is deadly, and what keeps a person alive in it are the customs said to grant safe passage through its wilds and the Faunus's territories: which stones to stack and for whom, which words go unspoken after dark, which ground belongs to a Faunus and must not be broken. The missionary recorded that this knowledge is the Fanewick's most prized possession, hoarded and traded between families, and that its keepers, who hold the old deals and observances, are among the wealthiest and most powerful Wicklings.

## The named Faunus

The missionary's records named dozens; Althan scholarship has kept only a handful, and disputes the details of those. Each is described with its own nature, and since the blight its own stance on the ruin:

- **Nikta, the Shepherd of the Seasons**, the Great Owl who turns the year with her two eyes, greatest of the Faunus.
- **Fulg, the Stacker of Stones**, who favors stacked river rocks and grows the shellfish, content to know little.
- **Hyacynis, They Who Spread Small Blossoms**, friend of bees and small flowers, who welcomed the blight as their domain swelled.
- **Ikla, the Sky Painter**, who colors the rare sunrise and sunset and is idle and sour between them.
- **Oove, the Watcher of the Night Bloom**, never seen, only heard whispering the night-blooming flowers awake, and said to watch from the moon.
- **Qui'Gar, Whispered Sweetness for a Thorny End**, who presides over the deaths that happen among the thorns, her quiet undone by the Witherwild.
- **Rohkin, the Wandering Horn**, warden of the horned herds, who has watched them warp into monsters.

Not all the Faunus are held to still live. The greatest of the dead is **Shun'Aush the Granite Ophid**, a vast serpent-Faunus said to have been slain in ages past, whose petrified body lies across the land like a range of grey stone. The Wicklings are recorded holding that ground sacred and cursed and warning against breaking it; [[haven|Haven]] was raised from it regardless.
