**Summary**: What little Althan scholars understand of the Wickling faith of the Witherwild, whose people share their land with the Faunus, beings they hold to be gods. Althan scholars grant them no more than this: they are creatures far greater than the ordinary life of the Fanewick. Reconstructed from one Haven-era missionary's records and the accounts of Witherwatch veterans, and disputed as much as it is cited.

---

Althan scholarship of the Fanewick faith rests on almost no first-hand material. Its one real source is a single [[house-aquila|House Aquila]] missionary who served in the [[haven|Haven]] colony and, bearing the [[miracles|Miracle of Pentecost]], could understand the Wicklings and record the beings they worship in words an Althan could read. That missionary named those beings the **Faunus** and fixed the forms of their names the mainland now uses; the Wicklings call them otherwise, in Wick. Those records, supplemented by the less reliable reports of [[the-witherwatch|Witherwatch]] veterans, are effectively the whole archive, and modern scholars weigh even them with caution. What follows is the reconstruction drawn from that thin and much-disputed record, and no scholar would call it settled.

## What the Wicklings call gods

The Wicklings hold the Faunus to be gods, and Althan scholars are careful not to. What the Faunus truly are, none will say; that they are something far greater than the ordinary life of the Fanewick, none disputes. By the records they are anything but distant: they walk the land in the flesh, dwelling in the villages and met on the road as readily as any neighbor. A few great Faunus are attested across the whole country, chief among them the Great Owl Nikta, the Shepherd of the Seasons; the missionary held that every community, and even every family, keeps its own lesser Faunus besides, a guardian of that hearth or that stretch of ground, unknown ten miles away. On their number the sources give no consistent answer.

## The push and the pull

Worship, by the missionary's account, is a bargain worked from both sides. The Faunus court the Wicklings' devotion with small miracles and safe passage through their territories, and repay those who slight or trespass on them with tricks and curses. The Wicklings depend on those blessings to live in a land that would otherwise kill them, and render each Faunus its due, the offering and the observance it expects, tuned to its own nature. Scholars note what the faith lacks by Althan measure: no high power set above the rest to adore, and no priesthood to stand between worshipper and worshipped. A Wickling deals with the Faunus directly, because the Faunus are there to be dealt with.

## The rules that keep them safe

What an Althan would name piety, the sources suggest a Wickling would name knowing the rules. The Fanewick is deadly, and the customs said to grant safe passage through its wilds and the Faunus's territories, which stones to stack and for whom, which words go unspoken after dark, which ground belongs to a Faunus and must not be broken, are what keep a person alive in it. The missionary recorded that this knowledge is the Fanewick's most prized possession, hoarded and traded between families, and that its keepers, who hold the old deals and observances, are among the wealthiest and most powerful Wicklings.

## The named Faunus

The missionary's records named dozens; Althan scholarship has kept only a handful, and disputes the details of those. Each is described with its own nature, and since the blight its own stance on the ruin:

- **Nikta, the Shepherd of the Seasons**, the Great Owl who turns the year with her two eyes, greatest of the Faunus.
- **Fulg, the Stacker of Stones**, who favors stacked river rocks and grows the shellfish, content to know little.
- **Hyacynis, They Who Spread Small Blossoms**, friend of bees and small flowers, who welcomed the blight as their domain swelled.
- **Ikla, the Sky Painter**, who colors the week's lone sunrise and sunset and is idle and sour between them.
- **Oove, the Watcher of the Night Bloom**, never seen, only heard whispering the night-blooming flowers awake, and said to watch from the moon.
- **Qui'Gar, Whispered Sweetness for a Thorny End**, who presides over the deaths that happen among the thorns, her quiet undone by the Witherwild.
- **Rohkin, the Wandering Horn**, warden of the horned herds, who has watched them warp into monsters.

Not all the Faunus are held to still live. The greatest of the dead is **Shun'Aush the Granite Ophid**, a vast serpent-Faunus said to have been slain in ages past, whose petrified body lies across the land like a range of grey stone. The Wicklings are recorded holding that ground sacred and cursed and warning against breaking it; [[haven|Haven]] was raised from it regardless.
