**Summary**: The arm of [[the-holy-see|the Holy See]] that presides over doctrine, scripture, and the proper form of worship. Where [[parish-of-inquisition|the Inquisition]] judges sin, Orthodoxy defines it, and guards the sacred words that Splendor magic itself depends on.

## Overview

The Parish of Orthodoxy is the doctrinal heart of [[the-holy-see|the Holy See]]. It settles what scripture means, what worship should look like, and where the line between faith and heresy falls. Most of the faithful meet it only through its rulings, handed down and enforced by other Parishes, but every one of those rulings begins here.

## Structure & Governance

### The Pontiff

The faith's supreme authority is the Pontiff, chosen by election of the Church's clerics and, by long habit, from among the deepest scholars of Orthodoxy. Election has never been blind to blood: a few houses carry [[miracles|Miracles]] so suited to the office that their names recur across the roll of Pontiffs. The chair is held now by [[amalthus-cruoris|Amalthus Cruoris]], a scriptural scholar of rare depth raised from Orthodoxy's own ranks and still its foremost voice, and bearer of his house's [[miracles|Miracle of Viaticum]].

## Beliefs & Purpose

### Keepers of the Word

Orthodoxy's quiet power comes from what it holds. [[splendor-magic|Splendor Magic]] is not a caster's own strength but a request answered by [[the-one-above|the One Above]], and the words that make a request work are fixed, ancient, and few. They are discovered, not invented, and most of them sit in the Parish's keeping, in scripture the Parish alone is trusted to study and decipher. Whoever controls that research controls what Splendor magic can even do. It is a slow, jealous kind of authority, measured in decades of decoding rather than open displays of force, and it makes Orthodoxy one of the most powerful arms of the Church without ever raising a hand.

### Commonprayer

The faithful pray by speaking the sacred words themselves. Orthodoxy holds, and any educated person knows, that a request is answered only in its exact and ancient form, the precise saying the One Above's power will heed (see [[splendor-magic|Splendor Magic]]). The common folk do not know this. They speak the words they have learned as best they can, trusting that to say them at all is to be heard, and where a priest's words take effect and a layman's do not, they lay it to the priest's blessing rather than to any matter of form. This everyday devotion is called commonprayer: the true words, said in the untaught way. Because the faithful hold that the One Above hears most clearly when many ask as one, commonprayer is most often made together, a household or a whole street lifting the same words at once.

### The covered eye

Orthodox doctrine teaches that mortal eyes should be covered before the divine, that to look on an angel unveiled is presumption. The teaching shapes the Parish's rites, its art, and the veils and masks its highest clergy wear in the presence of the sacred.

Once each week, on Dies Splendoris, the faithful gather to make their prayers in common, and the heart of that gathering is the Horacaecum, the Hour of Blindness. For that hour every eye is veiled and the congregation recites as one, waiting rather than mourning: no mortal sight is fit for the divine until the One Above returns, and only then will the veils be lifted and the faithful permitted to look at last. To keep the Horacaecum unveiled is to claim a worthiness no living person holds.

The fall of [[the-ophanim|the Ophanim]] on [[hilltop|Hilltop]] has made the doctrine a fault line rather than a comfort: some now ask whether the angel was a herald to be beheld or a judgment no one should have survived seeing.
