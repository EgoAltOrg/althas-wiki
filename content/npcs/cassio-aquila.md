**Summary**: Cassio Aquila, a Saint and scholar of [[the-holy-see|the Holy See]] and twin brother to [[lyra-aquila|Lyra Aquila]] of [[polaris|Polaris]]. One of the [[the-five-heroes|Five Heroes]], bearer of the [[miracles|Miracle of Pentecost]], he died bringing down [[the-ophanim|the Ophanim]] at [[hilltop|Hilltop]] in 334 VR.

---

## Abilities

### His Miracles

Cassio was born with the [[miracles|Miracle of Pentecost]]: he understood in full any word he heard and any notation he read, in any tongue, cipher, or voice. In a scholar-Saint of the [[splendor-magic|Splendor]] faith the gift was formidable, the scriptures lay open to him as plainly as speech, and no tongue or record of the Church was closed to his reading. Because he and his twin [[lyra-aquila|Lyra]] were each born with a different Miracle, he also held her [[miracles|Miracle of Anamnesis]] as fully as his own, and forgot nothing he had learned.

## History

Cassio Aquila was a Saint and scholar of [[the-holy-see|the Holy See]] and, with his twin sister [[lyra-aquila|Lyra Aquila]], one of the two living bearers of [[house-aquila|House Aquila's]] Miracles. He was among the [[the-five-heroes|Five Heroes]] who fought [[the-ophanim|the Ophanim]] at [[hilltop|Hilltop]] in 333-334 VR, and he died in the fighting, alongside the Voldaen High Prince [[edrion-voldis|Edrion Voldis]].

### The Ophanim's dying words

Of all who stood at [[hilltop|Hilltop]] when the [[the-ophanim|Ophanim]] fell, Cassio was one of the two who could understand the Herald's dying words, through the Miracle of Pentecost. The faith remembers him as a Saint who gave his life bringing the Ophanim down. His sister survived and set the account down in *Verba Ophanim*.

### The Pontiff who never was

The faith had all but marked Cassio for the Pontiff's chair, and he and his sister together, one voice raised in [[the-holy-see|the Holy See]] and one in [[polaris|Polaris]], were looked to as the pair who would draw church and magocracy into a new allied age. His death at [[hilltop|Hilltop]] ended that hope. The chair passed in time to [[amalthus-cruoris|Amalthus Cruoris]], whom the faith still reckons the lesser scholar of the two.
